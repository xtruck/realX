Face mesh prototype (steps S1-S8)

Shows the 468-point face mesh over your own camera picture, and can draw a photo of you on it. The camera picture is never read back, copied, stored or sent: it is shown here, given to the face model, and, while "Colour match" is above 0, to the graphics card only, so the photo takes your face's colour and light. Your photo stays in this page too: it is not uploaded or saved. Use a photo of yourself only; a "Virtual face" label stays on screen while a photo is drawn.

Whole head (off by default): hair, ears, the top of the forehead and the neck also come from your photo, softly blended. It loads a 16 MB model that cuts your photo (not the camera) into hair, skin and background once, and may cost some speed: watch the frames per second.

Hair that moves (off by default): the hair of your photo hangs from the head, swings when you move, and your hands can push it. It cuts the hair out of your photo (not the camera) once and loads the hand model, so watch the frames per second. It works best with a clear, front-facing photo of someone with long hair. It moves like a soft sheet of hair, not single strands, and only the hair that shows in the photo.

Show my hands on top (off by default): your own live hands, and what is in them, are drawn over the photo's body, so a bottle in your hand stays visible. It uses the hand model, and it sends your live picture to the graphics card for this, the same way colour matching does: it is only drawn on this screen, never read back, saved or sent. Only the area around your hands is shown; Hand reach makes that area bigger or smaller. When your hand is curled around something, Grip reach stretches the area along your knuckles, where a bottle or a cup usually sits: this is a guess, not detection (0 turns it off), and it also shows whatever else is in that area. Your hand keeps your own skin colour.

Spills, stains and eating (S10g): Spill on me puts a spill on the photo's shirt (it needs Body and clothes); it sticks to the shirt as you move, drips, and water dries on its own. Pass your hand over a stain to wipe it, or press Clean. Messy eating drops crumbs, and sometimes a stain, when your mouth opens with a hand near it. These are effects on the picture; they do not know what you really pour or eat.

Body and clothes (off by default): the shirt and sleeves of your photo follow your shoulders and arms and are sized to you. It cuts the body and clothes out of your photo (not the camera), finds the photo's shoulders once, and runs the body model on the live video about 14 times a second, so watch the frames per second. It works best when your photo shows the shoulders. It is a flat cut-out bent along the arms, not a 3D body. Cover my clothes draws a slightly bigger copy of the photo's shirt underneath to hide the edges of your own clothes (0 turns it off); it does not repaint the background behind you, so a big cover looks like a puffy shirt.

Match the webcam's look: the photo is softened, given your webcam's grain and nudged towards its contrast and white balance, so it looks filmed by the same camera. Measured on the graphics card from your live picture; 0% draws the photo as it is. It never changes the photo's skin colour by more than a few percent.

Hair reach (with Whole head): how far the photo's head reaches above your face. Raise it when more of your own hair shows round the photo's hairstyle — the photo's hair is drawn taller to cover it. It changes nothing below the ears.

Mouth patch is how much of your live mouth shows when it is open. Lower values pull the patch in towards the middle, which stops your own lips reading bigger and pinker than the photo's; 0% restores the full patch.

Use this photo?

Photos at several angles (off by default): add photos of yourself with your head turned 15° to 45° to one side. When you turn your head, your face is drawn from the side photo nearest your turn, so side views stretch less. Each side photo needs its own consent, like any photo, and photos turned more than 45° are refused. Only the face changes; hair, ears, neck and body still come from your front photo. Side photos load one at a time. It is a prototype: watch the frames per second, and leave it off if the 20 fps gate is not met.
