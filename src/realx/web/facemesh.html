Face mesh prototype (steps S1-S8)

Shows the 468-point face mesh over your own camera picture, and can draw a photo of you on it. The camera picture is never read back, copied, stored or sent: it is shown here, given to the face model, and, while "Colour match" is above 0, to the graphics card only, so the photo takes your face's colour and light. Your photo stays in this page too: it is not uploaded or saved. Use a photo of yourself only; a "Virtual face" label stays on screen while a photo is drawn.

Whole head (off by default): hair, ears, the top of the forehead and the neck also come from your photo, softly blended. It loads a 16 MB model that cuts your photo (not the camera) into hair, skin and background once, and may cost some speed: watch the frames per second.

Hair that moves (off by default): the hair of your photo hangs from the head, swings when you move, and your hands can push it. It cuts the hair out of your photo (not the camera) once and loads the hand model, so watch the frames per second. It works best with a clear, front-facing photo of someone with long hair. It moves like a soft sheet of hair, not single strands, and only the hair that shows in the photo.

Show my hands on top (off by default): your own live hands, and what is in them, are drawn over the photo's body, so a bottle in your hand stays visible. It uses the hand model, and it sends your live picture to the graphics card for this, the same way colour matching does: it is only drawn on this screen, never read back, saved or sent. Only the area around your hands is shown; Hand reach makes that area bigger or smaller, and an object much bigger than your hand is cut off at its edge. Your hand keeps your own skin colour.

Body and clothes (off by default): the shirt and sleeves of your photo follow your shoulders and arms and are sized to you. It cuts the body and clothes out of your photo (not the camera), finds the photo's shoulders once, and runs the body model on the live video about 14 times a second, so watch the frames per second. It works best when your photo shows the shoulders. It is a flat cut-out bent along the arms, not a 3D body, and it does not yet hide your own clothes underneath.
